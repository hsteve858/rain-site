Where does the rain go?

A two-week unit on how water moves between Earth's water sources and the sky — and how that movement drives the weather outside the classroom window.

Colorado Academic Standard, Grade 5, Standard 3 — Earth and Space Science. Evidence Outcome 5-ESS2-1: develop a model using an example to describe ways the geosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere, and/or atmosphere interact, limited to two systems at a time.

Unit focus

The hydrosphere–atmosphere interaction: how water moves between Earth's water and the air, and how that connects to weather.

"I can build a model showing how water moves between Earth's water and the sky, and explain how that connects to weather."

Unit learning toolkit NotebookLM: Where Does the Rain Go?

Two-week schedule

Three in-person days and two remote days each week. In-person days carry anything hands-on or messy — labs, model building, group discussion. Remote days carry content delivery and lower-stakes independent practice. Adjust the pattern to match your building's hybrid calendar.

Week One

Day 1 In-person Hook + pre-assessment KWL chart; "mystery puddle" class discussion — where did that water come from, where's it going?
Day 2 Remote Foundational content 6-minute video on states of water + PhET "States of Matter" simulation.
Day 3 In-person Hands-on lab Water cycle in a bag. Full lesson plan →
Day 4 Remote Vocabulary + check Digital vocab game (evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection) + Google Form check.
Day 5 In-person Discussion + model draft Small groups label a large water-cycle diagram poster; teacher circulates for misconceptions.

Week Two

Day 6 Remote Weather connection Short video linking water cycle to weather; weather journal entry using local Colorado Springs data.
Day 7 In-person Lab Cloud-in-a-jar or simple barometer build; observe and record.
Day 8 Remote Data practice Students track a live local forecast for 3 days, logging temperature and precipitation.
Day 9 In-person Model building Final project work time — students build their two-system-interaction model.
Day 10 In-person Presentations + assessment Share models in small groups; exit ticket + performance assessment. Assessment details →
